# ClaimTrail v0.4.0: Competitor Analysis

Phase 0, Business Validation. Researched 2026-11-23 from public sources
listed in §11. Figures marked **[sourced]** come from the cited source;
figures marked **[estimate]** are ClaimTrail's own arithmetic on sourced
inputs and are hypotheses to be tested in v0.2.0 and v0.5.0, not facts.
Nothing in this document is based on interviews: no customer has been
spoken to yet. That is v0.2.0's job, still deferred pending Aqib's help
with outreach.

This version re-verifies the four products from the 2026-11-14 DockClock
competitor check (GetDockPay, TruckersClaims, Detention Buddy, DOCK2BILL)
live, re-checks the TMS detention features named in v0.1.0 (Tezify,
TenTrucks), and runs a fresh structured sweep for carrier-desk claim
tools and new detention apps. Three products found in this sweep were
not in the November 14 check: Triporto, Eden One and Run Your Rig.
ClaimTrail's own side of every comparison comes from the master
checklist as verified through v1.1.3 and the v0.3.0 workflow validation,
not from new code reading, except where §6 says otherwise.

## 1. What changed since 2026-11-14

Nine days is a short interval, but the field moved in ways that matter:

- **GetDockPay shipped v1.1.** The Play Store listing (updated
  November 5, 2026) adds direct deposit: brokers can pay a driver's
  invoices online. The free plan now includes the invoice PDF, weekly
  and tax reports, and the advance broker notice; Pro ($24.99/mo or
  $239.99/yr, unchanged) adds auto-detect arrival, the live dock
  timer, ELD log attach, emailed invoice and numbered follow-up
  notices on day 3, 7 and 14, and the full Broker and Dock Scores
  directory. The site still says, verbatim, "Built for
  owner-operators, leased-on drivers and small fleets. Not
  dispatchers."
- **TruckersClaims now prices Pro.** On November 14 Pro was "still
  being built" with no price. Today the site lists Pro at $29/mo per
  truck (unlimited claims, 30-day follow-up lists, broker memory,
  fleet dashboard), while the FAQ still says "Pro is a paid plan
  we're still building, when it launches, building claims stays
  free." Read both together: the price is published, the product is
  not yet shipping. Free stays 3 claims a month, no card.
- **Detention Buddy is unchanged in substance.** The pricing
  discrepancy noted on November 14 is still on the page: the hero
  says Pro costs $7.99/month, the pricing section says $34.99/year
  (about $2.92/month). One new oddity: the "Real Results" section
  now shows $0+ recovered, $0+ hours documented and 0% claim success
  rate, while the hero still claims "$234,000+ in detention pay
  recovered". The zeros look like placeholder counters that never
  got wired to real data. Treat the $234,000 figure as self-reported
  and unverified, as before.
- **DetentionHero is still a waitlist.** Same early-access page, no
  pricing, no app-store listing found. It has not shipped in the
  nine days since the last check.
- **DOCK2BILL added a dispatcher partner program.** A new
  `/dispatchers` page offers dispatchers 20% of what each carrier
  they bring pays, every month, free; Partner Pro is $149/mo for up
  to 15 carriers ($299 above 15, founding partners $99/mo) for 25%
  commission, co-branding on packets and invoices, and a monthly
  per-carrier report. This is the first direct move by a rival into
  ClaimTrail's dispatch-agency beachhead, and it is a referral
  program, not a multi-tenant operations desk: the dispatcher does
  not run the carriers' claims inside DOCK2BILL.
- **Tezify published full pricing and deeper detention docs.**
  November 14 recorded "from $99/mo" from an older page. Today the
  site lists $49 for 1 truck, $99 for 2, $249 for 3-5, $399 for 6-10,
  $549 for 11-15, $799 for 16-25 and $32 a truck from 26 trucks, 30
  days free on up to 2 trucks, every feature on every plan. New help
  docs (updated November 13-18, 2026) describe a detention workflow
  that is deeper than v0.1.0 recorded: a detention calculator with
  separate customer and carrier rates, accessorial approval, carrier
  detention requests with receipt requests and approve/decline,
  automated detention and layover notices to the broker, and a
  collections and AR aging workflow.
- **Three new products surfaced in the sweep:** Triporto (driver
  detention app, early access, free during beta), Eden One (office
  product for small carriers with a detention workflow, founder
  pricing from $69/mo), and Run Your Rig (mobile-first owner-operator
  TMS with AI rate-con parsing and per-second detention, $9.99 per
  driver per month, 90 days free, web dashboard live, phone apps
  coming soon). Eden One and Run Your Rig are the most significant
  new finds in this version; both are profiled in §3.

## 2. Direct competitors: driver detention apps

These products serve the driver, not the office. ClaimTrail is a
desk product, so they are not direct competitors for the same buyer,
but they set the price anchor every buyer has already seen, and
they own the GPS time capture ClaimTrail does not have.

### 2.1 GetDockPay: the most complete driver app

- Site: https://getdockpay.com/, opened and read 2026-11-23, live.
  Also the Google Play listing, updated November 5, 2026.
- What it does: tap Arrived, Dock In, Departed; each tap stamps
  time and GPS. Free-time window and hourly rate produce the
  detention owed; a claim packet (timestamped log, GPS coordinates,
  BOL and photos, ELD log on Pro) is emailed to the broker. Pro
  sends automatic follow-up notices on day 3, 7 and 14, and tracks
  claims as submitted, paid or denied. Two community scores built
  from filed claims: Dock Score (how long a facility makes drivers
  wait) and Broker Pay Score (what share of a broker's claims get
  paid, and how fast). Geofence arrival reminders and a live dock
  timer are Pro features. New in v1.1: direct deposit so brokers
  can pay invoices online.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: Free $0 (log stops, detention math, BOL
  photos, invoice PDF, advance broker notice, weekly and tax
  reports). Pro $24.99/mo or $239.99/yr.
- Who it is for: owner-operators, leased-on drivers and small
  fleets. Explicitly not dispatchers. A small fleet can put its
  company name, MC and remit-to details on invoices.
- Traction signals: live on the App Store and Google Play, version
  1.1, with guides and a public help section. No user counts or
  revenue published. The ATRI figures it cites (75% billed, 55%
  paid) match the v0.1.0 research.
- What it does not do: no multi-truck office view, no roles, no
  layover or TONU or lumper claim types as a workflow (detention
  only), no evidence requirements or readiness check, no deadlines
  beyond the day 3/7/14 notice schedule, no audit trail, no
  reporting beyond per-driver claims and the two scores.

### 2.2 TruckersClaims: free claim builder, Pro priced but not shipped

- Site: https://truckersclaims.com/, opened and read 2026-11-23,
  live.
- What it does: tap arrival (GPS-stamped), tap release, billable
  hours calculated in the increments on the rate con. GPS-verified
  times carry coordinates; hand-typed times are labelled
  DRIVER-REPORTED. A finished PDF claim is ready to email or send
  to a factoring company. It warns before free time ends and writes
  the broker notice. Claims and details stay on the driver's own
  phone; nothing is uploaded. Pro (priced, not yet launched) adds
  unlimited claims, a 30-day unpaid follow-up list with the
  follow-up already written, broker memory (rate and free time fill
  themselves in), and a dashboard ranking docks by wasted hours and
  brokers by payment speed.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: Free $0, 3 claims a month, one truck, no
  card. Pro $29/mo per truck, paid through Stripe, cancel any time.
  The page says Pro is "still being built" even as it lists the
  price; treat Pro as announced, not shipping.
- Who it is for: a single driver on their own authority, a factored
  driver, or a company driver reporting to a dispatcher. An
  independent developer, not a brokerage or factoring company.
- What it does not do: no office product, no multi-user anything,
  no layover/TONU/lumper workflow, no deadlines, no evidence
  requirements, no follow-up automation in the free product (the
  30-day list is a Pro feature, and Pro is not shipping).

### 2.3 Detention Buddy: the cheapest driver app

- Site: https://www.detentionbuddy.com/, opened and read
  2026-11-23, live.
- What it does: one tap to start tracking, GPS-stamped arrival,
  photos as evidence, detention calculated in real time,
  professional PDF invoice, works offline, earnings dashboard,
  broker rankings, CSV export, quick repeat loads.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: Pro $34.99/year in the pricing section
  (about $2.92/month); the hero on the same page says $7.99/month.
  Both figures are quoted because the page contradicts itself.
  7 days of Pro free, no card.
- Who it is for: individual drivers. No fleet or office tier.
- Traction signals: rated 4.9/5 from 156 ratings (shown on the
  page). The "$234,000+ recovered" claim in the hero sits next to a
  results section showing $0+ on every counter, which reads as
  placeholder data. Do not quote either figure as verified.
- What it does not do: everything beyond a single driver's
  detention invoice. No follow-up automation is described, no
  claim lifecycle, no office view, no other charge types.

### 2.4 DetentionHero: still a waitlist

- Site: https://detentionhero.com/, opened and read 2026-11-23,
  live.
- What it does, as described: tap Arrived at the guard shack (GPS
  and timestamp locked), tap Departed when the BOL is signed, PDF
  invoice with rate-con reference, GPS data and total owed, emailed
  to the broker before leaving the lot. The pitch is GPS proof that
  stands up when a shipper will not sign an in-time.
- Pricing: none published. The page is an email capture for early
  access ("Be one of the first drivers"). No app-store listing was
  found in this sweep.
- Assessment: not a shipping product yet. Count it as a signal that
  the driver-app tier keeps attracting new entrants, not as a live
  rival. Re-check in v0.5.0 or later.

### 2.5 Triporto: new entrant, early access

- Site: https://triporto.com/, opened and read 2026-11-23, live.
  Operated by Entalabs LLC.
- What it does, as described: automatically logs GPS-verified
  arrival and departure, live detention timer, instant PDF invoice,
  one-tap email to the broker, earnings dashboard, and a fleet
  management view where operators manage multiple drivers, review
  detention claims and track earnings from one dashboard. It also
  targets dispatch teams with real-time load status and broker
  communication history. This is the first driver app in this set
  to advertise a fleet dashboard and a dispatcher view on its
  landing page.
- Pricing: "Free during beta. No credit card required." No paid
  price published. The page is a waitlist ("Now accepting early
  access", "We'll reach out as soon as early access opens").
- Assessment: not shipping yet, and the fleet and dispatcher
  features are claims on a landing page, not a product you can open.
  But it is the clearest sign that rivals are moving up from the
  single driver toward the small-fleet desk ClaimTrail targets. If
  Triporto ships its fleet dashboard, the "driver app" tier stops
  being a clean separate tier. Re-check before v0.5.0.

## 3. Direct competitors: the carrier and dispatcher desk

This is ClaimTrail's tier: products an office, a dispatcher or a
small carrier uses to turn waiting time into billed and collected
money. Three products sit here, and one of them (Eden One) was not
in the November 14 check at all.

### 3.1 DOCK2BILL: the closest match, now courting dispatchers

- Site: https://dock2bill.com/ and https://dock2bill.com/dispatchers
 , both opened and read 2026-11-23, live.
- What it does: import loads by CSV and keep your TMS. The driver
  gets one text link per load, taps "I'm here" and "Leaving now",
  photographs paperwork; no app and no account. The system checks
  every document against what that customer requires before billing
  (missing, unreadable or wrong-load documents are caught). A
  detention or TONU invoice is worked out from the times and the
  rate confirmation, proof attached, followed up if unpaid.
  Detention is billed in 15-minute steps from server time, not
  phone time, and the first tap counts. The office can fill in a
  stop the driver missed. It explicitly does not verify signatures
  and says so in writing.
- Pricing **[sourced]**, unchanged since November 14: Solo $99/mo
  (up to 5 trucks, 150 loads/mo, 2 users), Fleet $199/mo (up to 15
  trucks, 500 loads/mo, 5 users), Yard $299/mo (up to 30 trucks, no
  load cap, 10 users). Annual is two months free. No payment until
  the first load bills clean; cancel within 30 days of a payment
  for a full refund.
- The dispatcher program (new since November 14): Partner is free
  and pays 20% of what each referred carrier pays, every month they
  stay, with a list of carriers showing billing readiness and
  subscription status. Partner Pro is $149/mo up to 15 carriers
  ($299 above 15; founding partners $99/mo) for 25% commission,
  co-branding ("Your dispatcher: you, powered by DOCK2BILL") on
  packets, invoices, driver links and emails, a monthly per-carrier
  report (billed, detention recovered, days to invoice), and a
  carrier packet kit with expiry reminders.
- Who it is for, verbatim: "Carriers running five to thirty trucks
  that bill their own freight, or the dispatcher who does it for
  them."
- What it does not do: no layover or lumper claim workflow is
  described (detention and TONU only); no evidence requirements or
  readiness checklist as a product concept (its document check is
  per-customer required documents, which is adjacent but not the
  same); no claim lifecycle with acceptance, rejection, partial
  approval or payment tracking as named states; no deadlines with
  reminders beyond following up unpaid invoices; no reporting
  beyond the partner monthly report; no multi-tenant agency desk
  (the partner sees a list of carriers, not their operations).
- Assessment: still the closest single rival to ClaimTrail as a
  carrier product, and now the only rival with an explicit
  dispatcher channel. Its price ($99 for 5 trucks) is roughly
  double ClaimTrail's planned Growth tier ($49, unvalidated), and
  its pay-only-when-a-load-bills-clean model is a stronger
  risk-reversal than any subscription ClaimTrail plans. Its gaps
  are ClaimTrail's openings: layover and lumper, evidence
  requirements and readiness, the full claim lifecycle, deadlines,
  and real multi-tenancy.

### 3.2 Eden One: the new office-side rival

- Sites: https://www.edenone.app/product and
  https://www.edenone.app/pricing, both opened and read
  2026-11-23, live.
- What it does: a full office record for a small carrier, from rate
  confirmation to driver settlement, on one load record. Rate
  confirmations are read from an uploaded PDF (broker, rate, stops,
  detention terms) with human confirmation of each field. Dispatch
  board with every stop, each in its own time zone, with Arrived
  and Departed times. Detention workflow: set terms once (rate,
  free time, how time is billed, when the clock starts, a cap per
  stop), notify the broker on arrival and when free time runs out,
  send the claim as a PDF with times and proof of delivery, record
  what the broker approves, bill it on the invoice, and pay the
  driver for the wait. Invoicing only goes out with its rate
  confirmation and proof of delivery attached; approved detention
  is its own line; unbilled, sent and unpaid are tracked, with
  which brokers take longest to pay. Settlements are built from
  delivered loads. Documents and compliance (authority, BOC-3, W-9,
  insurance, MCS-150, driver qualification files) with expiry
  dates. Roles: Owner, Dispatcher, Accountant, Safety manager, each
  with defined reach. CSV/XLSX import with duplicate protection,
  free migration done by their team. No hardware; keep your ELD.
  A public user guide documents the whole product step by step.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: by fleet size, every feature in every tier,
  users not counted. Founder pricing for subscriptions started by
  December 31, 2026: 1-5 trucks $69/mo, 6-15 trucks $169/mo, 16-30
  trucks $329/mo; standard pricing $89/$189/$349. Annual is ten
  times monthly. 10-day trial, no card. Migration free.
- Who it is for: asset-based small carriers running their office on
  one record. The product page speaks to one carrier's office, not
  to an agency managing many carriers.
- What it does not do: no GPS capture (times are typed or come from
  the office record; it keeps your ELD but does not claim ELD-based
  arrival detection), no layover/TONU/lumper claim workflow named
  as such (detention is the named workflow; other accessorials are
  invoice lines), no evidence requirements or readiness checklist,
  no claim lifecycle with rejection and partial approval states, no
  configurable deadlines with reminders, no multi-tenancy, no
  audit trail as a named feature (the activity log exists in plain
  words: who, what and when, nothing editable).
- Assessment: Eden One is the strongest new rival in this version.
  It is a shipping product with public pricing, a public guide, and
  a detention workflow that covers the broker notice, the claim
  PDF, approval recording and invoicing, which is more of the claim
  lifecycle than any TMS in v0.1.0 offered. It is also a full office
  system (dispatch, invoicing, settlements, compliance, IFTA
  preparation), so a carrier that buys Eden One has less reason to
  buy a standalone claim tool. Two things keep it from covering
  ClaimTrail's ground: it is one carrier's office, not a
  multi-tenant desk, and its detention workflow is a path through
  invoicing, not a claim object with its own lifecycle, evidence
  requirements and deadlines. Its founder pricing ($69 for 1-5
  trucks) is above ClaimTrail's planned Starter ($19) and Growth
  ($49) tiers, which leaves ClaimTrail a price wedge at the small
  end, if the price is validated.

### 3.3 Run Your Rig: owner-operator TMS with AI rate-con parsing

- Sites: https://runyourrig.com/ and
  https://www.runyourrig.com/features, both opened and read
  2026-11-23, live.
- What it does: mobile-first TMS for owner-operators and small
  fleets, 1 to 1,000 trucks. Snap a rate confirmation (photo, PDF
  or pasted email) and it pulls 30+ fields (broker, rate, stops,
  detention terms, accessorials) in about three seconds, with
  tuned profiles for the big brokers. Detention by the second: tap
  in at the dock, tap out, GPS-verified check-ins, clock starts at
  the appointment for fixed appointments and tracks dwell only for
  first-come-first-served, auto-invoice on check-out, geofence
  auto check-in. Branded paperwork (rate con, BOL, lumper
  receipts, invoice) sent from the driver's own email or from Run
  Your Rig's servers with the driver's details. Facility memory
  (contacts, gate codes, average dwell per shipper). Document vault
  (MC authority, COI, W-9, NOA, carrier packet). Fleet dashboard on
  the web: smart inbox routing rate cons to drivers, email or code
  invites, per-driver revenue and dwell reports, roles (Owner,
  admin, dispatcher, driver). Everything works offline and syncs
  when signal returns.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: per driver per month, one subscription per
  fleet. Solo (1 driver) $9.99/driver/mo; Team (2-9 drivers)
  $8.99/driver/mo; Fleet (10+ drivers) $7.99/driver/mo. 90 days
  free, no card. A 5-driver fleet pays $44.95/mo; 12 drivers
  $95.88/mo.
- Status: the web dashboard is live now; the iOS and Android apps
  are "coming soon". So the detention flow is live on the web and
  the phone experience it is built around is not yet in the app
  stores. Note the gap between the marketing and the shipping
  state.
- What it does not do: no claim lifecycle beyond invoice and
  payment tracking on the load card (no acceptance, rejection,
  partial approval or follow-up states described); no evidence
  requirements or readiness; no deadlines; no layover, TONU or
  lumper as claim types (lumper receipts are attachments, not
  claims); no multi-tenancy (one fleet, one account).
- Assessment: the most feature-dense new rival per dollar, and the
  only one with AI rate-con parsing, which is the feature
  ClaimTrail's v0.1.0 research flagged as valuable and which no
  checklist version covers. Its $9.99/driver price undercuts every
  carrier-desk rival and sits below ClaimTrail's planned Starter
  tier for a one-truck buyer. Its weakness for ClaimTrail's purposes
  is that it is a driver-first TMS, not a claim desk: it automates
  the invoice, not the claim. And its phone apps are not shipping
  yet, so the core tap-in flow is web-only today.

## 4. Adjacent competitors: TMS suites with detention built in

### 4.1 Tezify TMS: deeper detention workflow than v0.1.0 recorded

- Site: https://www.tezify.us/ and help docs updated November
  13-18, 2026, home page and help pages read via search results
  2026-11-23 (the detention calculator, accessorial and
  carrier-request docs were read in full through search; the
  pricing page figures are from the home page).
- What it does, detention-related: a detention timer and detention
  billing on the load; a detention calculator with separate
  customer and carrier hourly rates, free hours, quarter-hour
  rounding; detention, lumper and layover as accessorials that need
  approval before they land on the invoice; carrier requests for
  detention or lumper logged under Carrier Pay with an "Ask for
  receipt" email, approve or decline, and a flag when a carrier
  bill has detention but no in/out times on the POD; automated
  detention and layover notices to the broker when a billable
  charge is approved; collections and AR aging with call logs,
  promises and reminders. Tezify is also a full TMS (dispatch,
  driver app, billing, driver pay, fuel, safety, maintenance,
  IFTA, settlements) and a brokerage TMS (rate cons, carrier pay,
  TONU handling on brokered loads, document requirements before
  billing).
- Pricing **[sourced]**: 30 days free on up to 2 trucks, then $49
  for 1 truck, $99 for 2, $249 for 3-5, $399 for 6-10, $549 for
  11-15, $799 for 16-25, $32 a truck from 26 trucks. Every feature
  on every plan, office logins not charged per seat, no contract.
  Tezify's own worked example: the 6-10 truck plan at $399/mo is
  covered by 7 billed detention hours at $60 an hour.
- Assessment: Tezify is the TMS whose detention workflow comes
  closest to a claim workflow: it has approval, receipt requests,
  a POD-times flag, broker notices and collections. What it does
  not have is a claim as an object: no claim lifecycle, no evidence
  requirements or readiness, no configurable deadlines, no
  multi-tenancy. Its price at 3-5 trucks ($249/mo) is five times
  ClaimTrail's planned Growth tier, and it is a whole TMS, so a
  carrier that already runs Tezify has little reason to add
  ClaimTrail for detention alone. A carrier that does not want a
  TMS is ClaimTrail's buyer, and Tezify's price is the argument
  for the standalone tool.

### 4.2 TenTrucks TMS: full TMS, no detention workflow called out

- Site: https://tentrucks.com/, pricing and feature pages read via
  search results 2026-11-23.
- What it does: all-in-one TMS (dispatch, live GPS, IFTA, invoicing
  with QuickBooks sync, driver settlements, driver app, document
  management, AI rate-con reading and error checks), its own
  FMCSA-registered ELD, optional human dispatching service at 4-6%
  per load. No detention-claim workflow is called out on the public
  pages reviewed; detention is not a headline feature.
- Pricing **[sourced]**: $145/mo for up to 5 trucks, $280 for 10,
  $540 for 20, every feature included, unlimited office users,
  month to month, no contract. (An older comparison page on the
  same site lists $129-$549 tiers; the pricing page figures above
  are the current ones.)
- Assessment: a full-TMS substitute, not a claim rival. A carrier
  on TenTrucks gets detention as an invoice line at best, and pays
  $145/mo for the privilege. The gap is the same as with Tezify:
  no claim lifecycle, no evidence requirements, no deadlines, no
  multi-tenancy. Its human dispatching service is a reminder that
  dispatch services are themselves a substitute for claim software.

### 4.3 Toro TMS, StackViro, Vektor: carried from November 14

## 5. Human substitutes

Carried from v0.1.0, unchanged, because no new evidence was sought
in this version:

- Dispatch services explicitly advertise fighting for detention,
  layover and TONU pay as part of back-office dispatch, typically
  at 5-10% of revenue. A carrier paying a dispatcher already has
  someone chasing detention, badly or well. ClaimTrail's pitch to
  that segment is the tool the dispatcher uses, not a replacement
  for the dispatcher. DOCK2BILL's partner program (§3.1) is the
  first product move that treats dispatchers as a channel rather
  than a competitor.
- Factoring companies chase unpaid invoices and some supply a
  paperwork app. Their app collects what the factor needs to
  advance the invoice; it does not run a claim lifecycle.
- No dominant, carrier-desk, multi-tenant, evidence-and-deadline
  claim platform was found in this sweep either. That negative
  result is now the third consecutive check (November 14, November
  21, November 23) with the same outcome, which strengthens it
  without proving it: absence of evidence is still not evidence of
  absence, and the sweep was English-language web search plus the
  sites named above, not an exhaustive market census.

## 8. Product gaps: what ClaimTrail lacks against this field

Ordered by how directly each gap decides a sale, not by how hard
it is to build. Each gap names the rivals that already have it.

1. **No submission channel.** ClaimTrail generates a PDF claim
   package and never sends it (v0.3.0 gap 1). Every shipping rival
   in this document sends the claim or invoice to the broker:
   GetDockPay Pro, TruckersClaims, Detention Buddy, DOCK2BILL,
   Eden One, Run Your Rig, Tezify. A desk product that cannot send
   the claim it builds is asking the buyer to do the last step by
   hand, which is the step the buyer is paying to avoid. This is
   the single most important gap in this version.
2. **No GPS time capture.** GetDockPay, TruckersClaims, Detention
   Buddy, DOCK2BILL, Run Your Rig and Triporto all capture
   GPS-stamped arrival and departure. ClaimTrail's times are typed
   in by hand (v0.3.0 gap 2). The broker dispute pattern in v0.1.0
   is that the shipper holds the time record; GPS capture is the
   feature that answers it. Without it, ClaimTrail's evidence is
   only as strong as the typed record, and the PDF package is a
   well-formatted version of an unverified claim.
3. **No driver capture flow at all.** There is no mobile app and no
   driver link. DOCK2BILL sends the driver one text link per load;
   Run Your Rig has a web dashboard with phone apps coming;
   Tezify and TenTrucks have driver apps. ClaimTrail's capture
   starts at the office keyboard. For a 2-20 truck fleet the
   dispatcher can type the times in, but for the agency beachhead
   the dispatcher is chasing drivers for times by phone, which is
   the workflow ClaimTrail is supposed to replace.
4. **No rate-con parsing.** Eden One reads rate-confirmation PDFs;
   Run Your Rig parses them with AI in about three seconds;
   TenTrucks and Tezify advertise AI rate-con reading and error
   checks. ClaimTrail has no rate-con input at all: free time,
   rates and terms are typed per claim or per setting, and v0.3.0
   recorded that no amount is calculated from the rate con. This
   is the highest-leverage automation gap after GPS capture,
   because the rate con is the document that decides what is owed.
5. **No broker or facility payment intelligence.** GetDockPay's
   Broker Pay Score and Dock Score are the reference implementation;
   Detention Buddy has broker rankings; Run Your Rig has facility
   memory and per-shipper dwell; Eden One tracks which brokers take
   longest to pay; TruckersClaims Pro promises a broker dashboard.
   ClaimTrail has reporting by broker in the reports module, but no
   payment-speed or facility-wait intelligence a dispatcher can use
   before booking a load. This was a hypothesis gap on November 14
   and is now a shipped feature in at least three rivals.
6. **No follow-up automation that sends.** ClaimTrail queues
   reminders (in-app plus email, verified in v1.0.1) but the
   follow-up schedule is a reminder to a human, not a notice to the
   broker. GetDockPay Pro emails numbered notices on day 3, 7 and
   14; DOCK2BILL follows up unpaid invoices; Tezify automates
   detention notices. The difference is who does the chasing.
7. **No payment collection or invoicing.** GetDockPay v1.1 added
   direct deposit; DOCK2BILL, Eden One, Run Your Rig and Tezify all
   produce invoices and track payment. ClaimTrail tracks payment as
   a claim status and records amounts, but it does not invoice and
   does not move money. This is a deliberate scope line (ClaimTrail
   is a claim tool, not a billing system), but it means the buyer
   still needs an invoicing step elsewhere, which weakens the
   "one place" pitch against Eden One and Tezify.
8. **No collections or AR aging.** Tezify has a collections workflow
   with call logs, promises and reminders. ClaimTrail has overdue
   indicators and outstanding-balance reports, but no collections
   workflow. A claim that is accepted but unpaid (v0.3.0 gap 6: no
   follow-up state for accepted-but-unpaid) is the exact case a
   collections workflow covers.
9. **No agency pricing or agency operations view.** See §7 point 2.
   DOCK2BILL's partner program is the only agency channel in the
   field, and it is referral, not operations. ClaimTrail's
   multi-tenancy is the operations answer, but without an agency
   plan or a per-carrier price the sales conversation has no
   number in it.
10. **Free tier too thin to evaluate.** See §7 point 3. Three days
    of history cannot contain a detention claim's life cycle
    (v0.3.0: claims are typically submitted within 24 hours and
    paid, if at all, weeks later). A buyer cannot see a claim
    through to payment inside the free tier.

Gaps that are NOT gaps, because a rival's version is weaker or
absent: evidence requirements and readiness (only DOCK2BILL's
per-customer document check is adjacent, and it is not a readiness
model); the 10-status claim lifecycle (no rival has one); deadlines
with reminders (no rival has configurable deadlines); multi-tenancy
(no rival has it); audit trail (only Eden One's plain-words log is
adjacent); layover and lumper as first-class claim types (only
Tezify's accessorials are adjacent, and they are invoice lines,
not claims).

## 9. Differentiation: revised scores and what is genuinely different

The November 14 check revised DockClock's differentiation to 3-4/10
as a driver app and 5-6/10 as a carrier desk. This version revises
the carrier-desk figure down, because the desk tier gained two
shipping rivals (Eden One, Run Your Rig) and one rival (DOCK2BILL)
opened a dispatcher channel since then.

| Positioning | Nov 14 score | Nov 23 score | Why it moved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Driver tap-and-invoice app | 3-4/10 | 3-4/10 | Unchanged. Five live or announced driver apps, two of them free to start. ClaimTrail is not a driver app and should not become one. |
| Carrier/dispatcher desk (claim workflow) | 5-6/10 | 4-5/10 | Eden One ships a detention workflow with broker notice, claim PDF, approval recording and invoicing; Run Your Rig ships detention automation at $9.99/driver; DOCK2BILL added a dispatcher partner program; Tezify's detention workflow is deeper than recorded in v0.1.0. The desk is no longer served by one rival. |
| Dispatch-agency desk (multi-tenant) | Not scored separately | 7-8/10 **[estimate]** | No rival offers multi-tenant operations: one account running many carriers' claims with per-carrier roles. DOCK2BILL's partner program is referral and reporting, not operations. This is the strongest remaining differentiation, and it is the v0.1.0 beachhead. It is scored as an estimate because no agency has validated it yet (v0.2.0). |

What is genuinely different about ClaimTrail, after this analysis:

1. **The claim as an object with a lifecycle.** Ten statuses,
   transitions with enforced rules (payment only via
   record-payment, rejection needs a reason, pre-submission close
   needs a reason, submitted is a one-way door), acceptance and
   rejection tracking, partial approval, payment tracking, status
   history. No rival models the claim this way; rivals model an
   invoice with a paid flag, or a load with an accessorial line.
   This is the product's core and it survives the comparison.
2. **Evidence requirements and readiness.** Configurable required
   and optional documents per claim, missing-evidence detection, a
   readiness checklist, authorized overrides. Only DOCK2BILL's
   per-customer document check is adjacent, and it checks documents
   before billing a load, not readiness of a claim. Note the honest
   caveat from v0.3.0: no default evidence requirements ship, so
   readiness reports 100% for a claim with no evidence unless the
   organization configures requirements. The engine is the
   differentiation; the defaults are a gap.
3. **Deadlines as a first-class concept.** Configurable deadlines
   with scope precedence, timezone-aware comparison, hourly
   refresh, automated reminders. No rival has configurable claim
   deadlines. This was a wedge in v0.1.0 and it still is.
4. **Multi-tenancy with RBAC.** Organizations, memberships,
   invitations, six roles, centralized permissions, strict tenant
   isolation verified by test. Eden One and Run Your Rig have
   roles inside one carrier; ClaimTrail has roles across many
   carriers in one account. This is the agency story.
5. **Charge-type breadth.** Detention, layover, TONU, lumper,
   additional stops, additional mileage and custom events as
   first-class claim types. Rivals are detention-first; only
   DOCK2BILL adds TONU and only Tezify adds the full accessorial
   set, as invoice lines.
6. **Price at the desk tier.** Planned Growth $49/mo is below every
   shipping desk rival. This is a wedge only if the price is
   validated (v0.5.0) and only if the buyer is comparing desk
   products.

What is NOT differentiation, and should not be claimed as such:

- GPS capture, detention math, PDF packages, broker notices,
  follow-up: all shipped by rivals, some of them free.
- "The dispatcher desk": DOCK2BILL serves it, Eden One serves the
  carrier office, Run Your Rig serves the fleet dashboard. The
  desk is served; the multi-tenant agency desk is not.
- "Cheapest": Detention Buddy at $2.92/mo and TruckersClaims Free
  are cheaper. ClaimTrail is cheapest among desk products, not
  cheapest overall, and the comparison a buyer actually makes
  depends on which tier they think they are buying.

The one-sentence pitch that survives this analysis: "ClaimTrail is
the desk where a dispatcher or an agency runs every detention,
layover, TONU and lumper claim for every carrier they serve,
through evidence, deadlines and payment, in one account." The
sentence is only true if the submission channel (gap 1) and GPS
capture (gap 2) are built, because a desk that cannot send the
claim or prove the time is a tracker, not a recovery tool. Both
belong on the roadmap before Phase 3 billing, not after it.

## 10. Implications for v0.2.0, v0.5.0 and the roadmap

### For v0.2.0 Customer Interviews (deferred, returns when Aqib is ready)

Add these questions to the 14 already recorded in v0.3.0:

1. Have you tried GetDockPay, Detention Buddy, TruckersClaims,
   DOCK2BILL, Eden One, Run Your Rig or Tezify's detention
   features, and why did you stop or not pay?
2. Who records your detention times today: the driver, the
   dispatcher, or nobody? Would a driver text link (DOCK2BILL
   style) work for your drivers, or do they not use their phones
   for paperwork?
3. Does your TMS already have a detention field, and what would a
   standalone claim tool have to do that the TMS field does not?
4. Would you pay $19-49/mo for the claim workflow if it could not
   send the claim to the broker for you? What if it could?
5. If you are a dispatch agency: how many carriers do you run
   claims for, and what would you pay per carrier per month for
   one account that runs all of them?
6. Do you check a broker's payment history before booking a load,
   and would a shared broker-pay score change which loads you
   take?

### For v0.5.0 Pricing Validation

- Test the Free/Starter/Growth tiers against the anchors in §7:
  $2.92/mo (Detention Buddy), $9.99/driver (Run Your Rig),
  $24.99/mo (GetDockPay Pro), $69/mo (Eden One founder), $99/mo
  (DOCK2BILL Solo), $249/mo (Tezify at 5 trucks).
- Define agency pricing explicitly: per-carrier price inside one
  agency account, or a flat agency tier. Do not take the agency
  beachhead into interviews without a number.
- Re-examine the Free tier: 3 days of history cannot contain a
  claim's life cycle. Compare 30-day (Tezify) and 90-day (Run Your
  Rig) trials and DOCK2BILL's pay-when-it-bills-clean model, and
  set the free offer so a buyer can see one claim through to
  payment before paying.
- Test the pay-when-it-bills-clean idea as an alternative to a
  free tier: it is the strongest risk reversal in the field and it
  aligns price with the recovery ClaimTrail promises.

### For the product roadmap (proposals, not decisions)

In priority order, from this analysis and v0.3.0's gaps:

1. **Submission channel** (v0.3.0 gap 1, §8 gap 1): send the PDF
   claim package to the broker by email from inside ClaimTrail,
   with the sent record on the claim. Every rival has this.
2. **GPS time capture** (v0.3.0 gap 2, §8 gap 2): a driver link or
   mobile flow that stamps arrival and departure with GPS, in the
   DOCK2BILL text-link style if a full app is too much. This is
   the feature v0.1.0 called the most valuable future feature; it
   is now also the most common rival feature.
3. **Rate-con parsing** (§8 gap 4): read free time, rates and terms
   from an uploaded rate confirmation, in the Eden One / Run Your
   Rig style. Removes the typing that makes claim creation slow.
4. **Default evidence requirements** (v0.3.0 gap 5): ship
   per-event-type defaults so readiness means something out of the
   box instead of reporting 100% with no evidence.
5. **Broker payment intelligence** (§8 gap 5): per-broker payment
   speed and success rate from the claims in the system, and
   per-facility wait history. Start with the carrier's own data;
   a shared score needs a user base first.
6. **Agency plan and pricing** (§7 point 2, §8 gap 9): an agency
   tier with per-carrier pricing, decided in v0.5.0, built when
   Phase 3 billing lands.

None of these is in the checklist yet. Proposing them here does not
add them to the checklist; that is Aqib's decision, and the GPS
capture proposal from v0.1.0 is still awaiting one. The honest
sequencing note: gaps 1 and 2 decide sales, so they should be
weighed against Phase 3 billing order when Aqib reviews this
version, not silently queued behind it.

## 12. Limitations of this research

- No interviews, no pilot customers, no primary data. Every
  conclusion about willingness to pay, workflow fit and price
  points is a hypothesis until v0.2.0 and v0.5.0.
- No product was signed up for, trialled or paid for. Features and
  prices are from public pages, not from using the products. A
  landing page can promise a feature that does not ship yet
  (Triporto's fleet dashboard, TruckersClaims Pro, Run Your Rig's
  phone apps, DetentionHero's whole product) and the document
  labels those cases where it found them, but it cannot catch
  every one.
- Tezify and TenTrucks were read through search results, not by
  opening the pages in full; their figures are less certain than
  the eleven pages opened directly. Toro TMS, StackViro, Vektor,
  DENEMO, AthenaIQ, Hemut and ezLoads are search-result or
  second-hand level. Do not quote those figures outside this
  document without opening the vendor's own page.
- Detention Buddy's page contradicts itself on price ($7.99/mo in
  the hero, $34.99/yr in the pricing section) and shows $0+ on
  every results counter next to a $234,000+ hero claim. Both
  figures are quoted and flagged, not reconciled.
- GetDockPay's Pro price is per account; whether a multi-driver
  fleet pays per driver is not stated on the pages read, so the
  5-truck and 10-truck columns for GetDockPay in §7 are marked as
  an assumption, not a sourced figure.
- The sweep was English-language web search plus the named sites.
  Products with no English web presence, products marketed only
  inside load boards or factoring portals, and products that do not
  call themselves detention or claim tools would not have been
  found. The "no dominant multi-tenant claim platform" finding is
  a sweep result, not a census.
- Competitor prices and features are from public pages accessed
  2026-11-23 and change without notice. Re-verify before quoting
  any of them in sales material or in v0.5.0.
