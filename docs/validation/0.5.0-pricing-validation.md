# ClaimTrail v0.5.0: Pricing Validation

Phase 0, Business Validation. Researched 2026-11-24 from public sources
listed in section 12. Figures marked **[sourced]** come from the cited
source; figures marked **[estimate]** are ClaimTrail's own arithmetic on
sourced inputs and are hypotheses to be tested in v0.2.0 interviews, not
facts. Nothing in this document is based on interviews: no customer has
been spoken to yet. That is v0.2.0's job, still deferred pending Aqib's
help with outreach. This version therefore validates pricing by proxy:
published competitor prices, published SaaS pricing benchmarks, the
value math of one recovered claim, and what buyers already pay for the
human substitutes. It does not claim to know what any carrier will pay.
Section 10 lists the exact questions interviews must still answer, and
the kill criteria that would force a re-price.

This version answers the four v0.5.0 checklist items and the three open
questions v0.4.0 left for it: agency pricing, the 3-day free tier, and
pay-when-it-bills-clean as an alternative to a free tier. It also
re-checks the two waitlist rivals from v0.4.0 (Triporto, DetentionHero)
and records two new pricing data points found in this pass (CabCommand
flat fleet tiers, 3 Lakes Logistics automated recovery at $300/mo).

## 1. The pricing problem, stated plainly

ClaimTrail's planned pricing comes from the RelayDeck-era plan, set
before any validation: Free $0 (1k events, 3 days), Starter $19/mo
(50k events, 30 days), Growth $49/mo (500k events, 90 days). Three
things are wrong with that plan as written, and this version fixes all
three as hypotheses, not decisions:

1. **The limits are in the wrong unit.** "Events" and "days of history"
   are internal units. A buyer cannot tell whether 50,000 events covers
   their fleet, and a 3-day history limit cannot contain a claim's life
   cycle (v0.4.0 section 7, point 3). Limits must be in buyer units:
   trucks, shipments, claims, members, storage.
2. **There is no agency price.** v0.4.0 showed the beachhead segment,
   dispatch agencies, has no number to buy at: 10 carriers at Growth
   $49/mo is $490/mo, against DOCK2BILL Partner Pro at $149/mo, which
   also pays the dispatcher a 25% commission. An agency cannot be sold
   a product with no agency price.
3. **The free offer is the thinnest in the field.** 3 days of history
   against 30-day trials (Tezify), 90-day trials (Run Your Rig), and
   DOCK2BILL's pay-only-when-a-load-bills-clean. A buyer cannot see one
   claim through to payment inside the free tier, so the free tier
   cannot do the one job a free tier exists to do: let the buyer
   experience the outcome.

The rest of this document is the evidence for the revised price points
in section 8, the subscription structure in section 9, and the interview
tests in section 10.

## 2. Pricing models researched

Six models, plus the hybrid and outcome variants that dominate current
B2B SaaS. The evidence is from published 2025-2026 surveys and
benchmark reports, not from ClaimTrail's own data, which does not exist
yet.

### 2.1 The six models and what the benchmarks say

| Model | Value metric | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat-rate | Access, one price | Predictable, caps growth. Basecamp charges $299/mo unlimited. IDC forecasts 70% of vendors will move away from flat-rate or pure per-seat by 2028. |
| Per-seat | Named or active users | Most common historically (67% of implementations in one 47-company analysis). Net revenue retention averages about 110%, against 130-160% for usage-based. |
| Tiered | Feature or limit bundle | Most widely used structure: 67% of SaaS companies use tiered packaging. Predictability high, scaling medium. |
| Usage-based | Units consumed | Highest NRR (130-160% reported) but lowest budget predictability. Needs metering and billing from day one. |
| Freemium | Free tier plus paid tiers | Median free-to-paid conversion 2-5% (OpenView), 2.6-3.7% (First Page Sage). Roughly 50% lower acquisition cost than trial-only, per ProfitWell 2026. |
| Hybrid | Base plus metered or outcome component | Now the most common model: 37% of 230 B2B software and AI companies in Kyle Poyar's 2026 survey, projected to reach 61% of SaaS companies by end of 2026 (Chargebee). |

Sources: getfairview.com SaaS pricing models compared (Chargebee State
of Subscriptions, OpenView); racetoscale.com (Benchmarkit: subscription
is the primary model for over 80% of B2B SaaS); ascentpractice.co
(Poyar 2026 survey of 230 companies; 75% changed pricing or packaging
in the previous year; only 5% of investors surveyed preferred
seat-based pricing); axis-intelligence.com (47 implementations across
5 companies).

### 2.2 What fits ClaimTrail

Three candidate value metrics were scored against the buyer, not
against SaaS fashion:

- **Per seat (per user).** Wrong for this buyer. A 5-truck carrier has
  one dispatcher and maybe one office person; a 20-truck carrier has
  two or three. Headcount barely moves while claim volume moves 20x.
  Per-seat pricing would charge the agency with 20 carriers and 2 users
  almost nothing, and the 1-truck owner-operator with a dispatcher the
  same as a 5-truck fleet. It also punishes the exact behaviour the
  product wants: adding the dispatcher, the billing person and the
  owner as separate roles. Every rival that charges per seat is a
  driver app (per driver) or a TMS with office logins included.
  Reject per-seat as the primary metric.
- **Per truck.** The metric the whole trucking market already uses.
  Tezify, Eden One, TenTrucks, DENEMO, AthenaIQ, DOCK2BILL, Run Your
  Rig (per driver) and TruckersClaims Pro (per truck) all price by
  fleet size. A buyer can compare instantly, and the metric scales
  with the value: more trucks means more loads, more detention events,
  more claims. The weakness is that a truck that never waits pays the
  same as a truck that waits every day, which is why per-truck alone
  under-prices the heavy user and over-prices the light one. Use
  per-truck as the tier boundary, not as a metered charge.
- **Per claim / per recovered dollar (outcome).** The metric that
  matches the promise: ClaimTrail earns when the carrier recovers.
  Only 5% of companies in Poyar's 2026 survey named outcome pricing
  as their primary model, and Euclid Ventures' 2026 analysis calls it
  bad advice for most vertical AI founders, because the vendor and the
  buyer must agree on what the result was worth and how much of it the
  vendor caused. That attribution problem is real for ClaimTrail too:
  a claim paid 60 days after submission was "caused" by the claim
  package, the follow-up, or the broker's own cycle, and the product
  cannot prove which. But the recovery-adjacent precedents in section
  6 show a hybrid version works: a base subscription plus a success
  fee on recovered money, with the fee defined on cash actually
  received. Keep outcome pricing as a hybrid component, not the
  primary model.

**Recommendation [hypothesis]: tiered pricing by fleet size (per
organization, with truck bands), plus a hybrid success-fee option.**
The base is a predictable monthly subscription the buyer can budget,
tiered by trucks because that is how the market already buys. The
success fee is an optional alternative for buyers who will not pay a
subscription before they have seen a recovery. Both are specified in
section 8; the subscription structure that supports them is in
section 9.

### 2.3 Annual billing

The field's annual terms, all **[sourced]** from v0.4.0 and this pass:

- DOCK2BILL: annual is two months free.
- Eden One: annual is ten times monthly (two months free).
- GetDockPay Pro: $239.99/yr against $24.99/mo, about 20% off (2.4
  months free).
- TruckersClaims Pro: monthly only, cancel any time.

Two months free is the field norm. ClaimTrail's annual price should be
10x monthly, no card required to start a trial, cancel any time on
monthly. Annual billing matters for a second reason in this segment:
churn. v0.1.0 recorded that small carriers fail often and change tools
often. A buyer who pays annually is a buyer who has decided; a buyer on
monthly is a buyer still deciding. The trial design in section 5 has to
earn the annual decision, not assume it.

## 3. The value math: what one recovered claim is worth

Pricing is only defensible if the value it charges against is stated
in the buyer's own numbers. All inputs here are sourced; the arithmetic
is ClaimTrail's and is labelled [estimate].

### 3.1 The claim values

- Detention rates: $50-75/hr **[sourced]** (v0.1.0 accessorial rate
  guides). Layover $250-350/day, TONU $200-300, lumper $150-250
  **[sourced]** (same).
- One collected detention charge: $75-300 **[sourced]** (CabCommand
  trucking software pricing comparison, July 2026 research, accessed
  2026-11-24). CabCommand's own framing, which is the right one:
  "If software reliably produces two of those a month, the
  subscription is a rounding error. If it can't, it's expensive at
  any price."
- DetentionHero's self-reported figures, new in this pass: $4,200
  average annual loss per owner-operator who stops filing, and 63%
  of brokers denying claims citing no shipper-signed in-time
  **[sourced, self-reported, unverified]**. Treat the $4,200 as a
  vendor claim, not a fact; it is consistent in direction with the
  ATRI figures in v0.1.0 but has no published method behind it.
- FF Dispatch, a dispatch service, illustrates detention recovery of
  +$300-500 in its monthly comparison of its own service against a
  one-man dispatcher **[sourced, vendor illustration]**. The number is
  marketing, but it is a dispatcher telling carriers what detention
  recovery is worth in a month, which is the number ClaimTrail's price
  is judged against.
- Tezify's own worked example **[sourced]** (v0.4.0): its 6-10 truck
  plan at $399/mo is covered by 7 billed detention hours at $60/hr.

### 3.2 Break-even arithmetic [estimate]

Using the sourced rate range and the $75-300 collected-claim range:

- At Growth $49/mo, one collected claim of $120 (2 hours at $60/hr)
  pays for about 2.4 months of subscription. One $75 claim pays for
  1.5 months. A carrier that collects one detention claim per quarter
  is roughly break-even on Growth; a carrier that collects one per
  month pays $49 to recover $75-300 and keeps the rest.
- At Starter $19/mo, one collected claim of $75 pays for about 4
  months. The Starter buyer needs one claim per quarter to be clearly
  ahead.
- At the agency price proposed in section 8 ($149/mo for up to 10
  carriers), one collected claim per carrier per quarter at $120
  each is $1,200/quarter recovered against $447/quarter paid, before
  the agency's own margin on the service.

The honest caveat, from v0.1.0: fewer than 50% of detention invoices
are paid today, and better paperwork alone may move that to 60%, not
100%. The break-even above assumes the claim is collected. A carrier
that files three claims and collects one has paid three claims' worth
of effort for one claim's worth of money, and the subscription is
charged whether the broker pays or not. That asymmetry is exactly why
the success-fee alternative in section 6 matters: it moves the
collection risk from the buyer to the vendor, which is the only honest
way to sell to a buyer who has been burned by unpaid invoices before.

### 3.3 What the buyer already pays for the substitutes

The price is never judged against zero. It is judged against what the
buyer pays today to get the same outcome, badly or well:

- **Dispatch services: 5-8% of gross linehaul** **[sourced]** (v0.1.0;
  confirmed in this pass: Areesit Logistics 5% flat on linehaul,
  accessorials 100% the carrier's; Lanewise Dispatch 5-7%; FF Dispatch
  5-8%). A truck grossing $20,000/mo pays $1,000-1,600/mo to a
  dispatcher **[estimate]** (CabCommand's worked example: $600-1,600
  on $20,000 gross). Detention chasing is included in that fee by the
  services that do it, and explicitly free of commission by FF
  Dispatch and Areesit. Against that, $19-49/mo for a tool is 2-5% of
  the dispatch fee. The pitch writes itself: the tool costs less than
  the dispatcher's detention work is already worth, and the dispatcher
  is the person who should be using it.
- **Driver apps: $2.92-29/mo** **[sourced]** (v0.4.0). The $2.92
  Detention Buddy price is the anchor that makes $49/mo look expensive
  to a buyer who thinks they are buying a timer. The answer is not to
  lower the price to $3; it is to make sure the buyer is comparing desk
  products, where the cheapest price is $49 (ClaimTrail) against $69
  (Eden One founder) and $99 (DOCK2BILL Solo). See section 4.
- **Full TMS: $49-799/mo** **[sourced]** (v0.4.0: Tezify $49-799,
  TenTrucks $145-540, DENEMO $50+, AthenaIQ $79+). A carrier already
  on a TMS has detention as an invoice line and no reason to add a
  standalone claim tool. ClaimTrail's buyer is the carrier not on a
  TMS, or the agency running carriers who are not.
- **Automated recovery platforms: $300/mo** **[sourced, new in this
  pass]**. 3 Lakes Logistics charges $300/mo founders ($500/mo Pro)
  for AI dispatch, compliance and automated detention/TONU/lumper
  recovery, claiming +$400/mo average recovered. It is a full
  automation stack, not a claim tool, and its price shows what a buyer
  will pay when recovery is bundled with dispatch: six times
  ClaimTrail's Growth tier. It also shows the ceiling: if ClaimTrail
  ever adds automated filing, the price conversation changes entirely.

Four readings from the table:

1. **ClaimTrail is still the cheapest desk price, and the gap is
   widening at the small end.** CabCommand, the closest pricing
   analogue (flat per-fleet tiers, no per-seat charge), charges
   $29.99 for one truck and $69.99 for 2-5 trucks. ClaimTrail's
   Starter at $19 and Growth at $49 undercut both. The wedge holds
   only for desk-to-desk comparison; against Run Your Rig at $9.99
   per driver, the buyer needs the one-sentence justification from
   v0.4.0 section 7: the claim workflow, not the timer.
2. **The per-truck price falls with fleet size everywhere except
   ClaimTrail's plan.** Tezify charges $49 per truck at 1 truck and
   $39.90 per truck at 10. Eden One charges $69 for 1-5 trucks and
   $16.90 per truck at 10. DOCK2BILL charges $19.80 per truck at 5
   and $13.27 at 15. ClaimTrail's flat $49 per organization means the
   per-truck price falls fastest of all, which is good for the 10-20
   truck buyer and leaves money on the table from the 2-5 truck buyer
   who would pay more per truck. The tier bands in section 8 fix this
   by charging more as the truck band rises, while keeping the
   per-truck price falling.
3. **The agency column is still empty except for DOCK2BILL.**
   DOCK2BILL Partner Pro at $149/mo for up to 15 carriers is the only
   published agency number in the field, and it is a referral program
   that pays the dispatcher, not an operations desk that charges the
   dispatcher. ClaimTrail's agency price must be set against $149/mo,
   not against $490/mo, because $490/mo is the price of not having an
   agency plan at all.
4. **3 Lakes Logistics at $300/mo is the price of automation.**
   It bundles dispatch, compliance and recovery and charges six times
   Growth. It proves a carrier will pay $300/mo for a system that does
   the work, not just tracks it. ClaimTrail today tracks the work; the
   $300 number is the prize for the submission channel and GPS capture
   on the v0.4.0 roadmap, not a price ClaimTrail can charge today.

## 5. Willingness to pay: what can be validated without interviews

No interviews exist, so no willingness-to-pay figure in this document
is a finding. It is a proxy, and each proxy is labelled as such.

### 5.1 Proxy 1: the buyer already pays 20-30x the price for the human version

A dispatcher costs 5-8% of gross linehaul, $1,000-1,600/mo per truck
at $20,000 gross **[estimate from sourced percentages]**. A factoring
company takes 1-5% of invoice value for advancing payment. A
dispatcher who chases detention is already being paid to do the job
ClaimTrail organises. The willingness-to-pay question is therefore not
"will a carrier pay $49/mo for software" but "will a carrier pay $49/mo
for the tool their dispatcher uses to do a job they already pay
$1,000/mo for". Framed that way, $49 is 3-5% of the existing spend on
the same outcome. The risk is not that $49 is too much; it is that the
carrier does not believe the tool changes the outcome, because fewer
than half of detention invoices are paid today and the tool cannot
force a broker to pay. That is a product-belief problem (submission
channel, GPS proof), not a price problem, and lowering the price does
not fix it.

### 5.2 Proxy 2: the free-to-paid math

If the free tier converts at the published freemium median of 2-5%
(OpenView; First Page Sage 2.6-3.7%), then 1,000 free organizations
produce 20-50 paying ones **[estimate]**. At $39/mo average, that is
$780-1,950/mo from 1,000 free signups. The trial model converts far
better: opt-in trials (no card) convert at a median of about 18.5%
(1Capture, 10,000+ SaaS companies, 2025), and card-required trials at
about 49% (First Page Sage, 86 SaaS companies) but with 65% lower trial
volume. ChartMogul's February 2026 survey of 200 B2B products is more
conservative: a median free-to-paid rate of 8%, with 4-6% rated good
for no-card trials and 25-35% good for card-required trials. Either
way, a time-limited trial converts roughly 3-6x better than a
permanent free tier. A permanent free tier also carries a support cost
in a segment where the buyer phones for help, and a free tier that
never expires is an invitation to use ClaimTrail as a free filing
cabinet. The trial benchmarks argue for a trial, not a free tier, as
the entry offer. Section 7 makes the recommendation.

### 5.3 Proxy 3: trial length against the claim life cycle

Trial-length benchmarks, all **[sourced]**:

- 1Capture (10,000+ SaaS, 2025): 7-day trials convert at a 24%
  median, 14-day at 19%, 30-day at 14%. Shorter converts better on
  median, because urgency concentrates activation.
- RevenueCat (17,000+ subscription apps, Aug 2025-Jul 2026, mobile
  apps, so read as directional for B2B): on monthly plans, conversion
  peaked at 46.6% on 10-16 day trials, 43.7% on 17-32 days; renewal
  kept rising with length, 77.5% first renewal after 17-32 day trials
  against 49.5% with no trial at all.
- ChartMogul (200 B2B products, Feb 2026): 62% of trials run 14 days,
  only 20% require a credit card.
- The general rule from the trial literature: trial length should be
  time-to-value times a safety factor, 7-14 days for most B2B
  software with active onboarding, 21-30 days for complex B2B.

ClaimTrail's time-to-value is not 7 days. A carrier signs up, imports
shipments, records a detention event, builds a claim, submits it, and
waits for the broker. Submission happens within 24-48 hours of
delivery (v0.3.0); payment, if it comes, comes weeks later. A 7-day
trial lets the buyer build a claim but never see it paid. A 14-day
trial lets the buyer build and submit, and see the follow-up reminders
work, but not see money. A 30-day trial lets the buyer see the first
claims submitted and followed up, and possibly the first fast payment.
The conversion benchmarks say shorter trials convert better in general;
the claim life cycle says ClaimTrail's buyer cannot experience the
outcome in a short trial. The two point in opposite directions, and the
life cycle wins, because a trial that converts a buyer who has not seen
the outcome produces a first-month churn, not a customer. Section 7
recommends 30 days, no card, on the Growth tier, with the success-fee
option in section 6 as the alternative for buyers who want to pay only
on recovery.

### 5.4 Proxy 4: the price the buyer names themselves

Two rival pages quote the buyer's own framing. CabCommand: "Price it
against outcomes, not features: one collected detention charge is
$75-$300." Tezify: 7 billed detention hours at $60/hr covers the
$399/mo plan. Both vendors sell the price by naming the recovered
amount that covers it. ClaimTrail's pricing page, when it is built in
v1.2.2, should do the same: Growth $49/mo is covered by one collected
detention hour at $60/hr, or one $120 claim every two months. That
framing is honest only if the product can show the buyer their own
recovered amount, which the reports module already does (recovered
amounts per month, by status). The pricing page should quote the
buyer's own numbers back to them, not a generic ROI claim.

### 5.5 What proxies cannot validate

Three things no proxy can settle, and which stay open until v0.2.0:

1. Whether a 2-20 truck carrier will pay $19-49/mo at all, or whether
   the $2.92 driver-app anchor has already set the price of "detention
   software" in their head at under $10.
2. Whether a dispatch agency will pay per carrier, per month, or a flat
   agency fee, and what that number is.
3. Whether the success-fee alternative is preferred to a subscription,
   or whether carriers distrust a vendor that takes a cut of their
   recovery.

Section 10 turns these into interview questions with kill criteria.
Until interviews answer them, every price in section 8 is a hypothesis
with a stated test, not a decision.

## 6. Pay-when-it-bills-clean and the success-fee alternative

v0.4.0 called DOCK2BILL's model the strongest risk reversal in the
field. This section tests it as an alternative to a subscription, and
as a hybrid alongside one.

### 6.1 The DOCK2BILL model, restated

DOCK2BILL charges nothing until a load bills clean, then the monthly
subscription starts; cancel within 30 days of a payment for a full
refund **[sourced]** (v0.4.0). The buyer pays only after the product
has done its job once. The model works for DOCK2BILL because its job
is per-load and fast: a load bills clean within days of delivery, so
the free period is short and the proof is immediate. ClaimTrail's job
is slower: a claim is built, submitted, and paid weeks later, so
"pay when it bills clean" would mean a free period of 30-60 days
before the first payment, and the buyer could use the product through
a whole claim cycle without paying. That is a longer free ride, but it
is also a truer proof: the buyer pays after seeing money, not after
seeing a form.

### 6.2 The success-fee precedents

A success fee on recovered money is an established model in
recovery-adjacent products, all **[sourced]** from this pass:

- **DueWell** (patient-balance recovery platform): platform fee of
  $500-2,000/mo plus a success fee of 15%, 10% or 8% of qualifying
  aged cash recovered, with the fee defined on patient-paid dollars
  net of refunds, inside a 12-month attribution window. The fee
  falls as the platform fee rises. The key design choice: the fee
  applies to cash actually received, never to a written-off portion,
  and the monthly statement shows the account-level detail so every
  fee is auditable.
- **ChurnBack** (SaaS churn recovery): $49/mo base plus 25% of
  revenue actually recovered through its cancel flows and dunning.
  If it saves $0, the buyer pays $49. The vendor states the
  crossover plainly: above about $3,800/mo recovered, the 25% fee
  exceeds a typical flat-fee tool.
- **Amazon shortage-claim recovery services**: contingency fees of
  15-25% of successfully recovered funds are the published norm
  (Wake Commerce ProfitGuard 15%, Eva Commerce 20%, RT7 Digital
  25%), billed after the money reaches the bank account. The
  contract risk the source stresses is the fee base: a percentage of
  new cash recovered because of the provider's work is very different
  from a percentage of every credit that appears during the contract.
- **Outcome pricing in general**: only 5% of companies in Poyar's
  2026 survey named it their primary model. Euclid Ventures' analysis
  warns that a share of value asks both sides to agree on what the
  result was worth and how much of it the vendor caused. Intercom's
  Fin charges $0.99 per resolved outcome, which is a fee for a
  completed job, not a share of value, and is the cleaner pattern.

### 6.3 The arithmetic for ClaimTrail [estimate]

At a 10% success fee on recovered money, with no base subscription:

- A carrier recovering $500/mo pays $50/mo, about the Growth price.
- A carrier recovering $200/mo pays $20/mo, about the Starter price.
- A carrier recovering $2,000/mo pays $200/mo, four times Growth,
  which is more than the flat subscription and is the point where the
  buyer starts to resent the fee. This is the ChurnBack crossover
  problem: a success fee punishes the buyer's success.
- A carrier recovering $0 pays $0, which is the risk reversal, and
  also the vendor's revenue problem: free users who never recover
  cost support and infrastructure and pay nothing.

At a hybrid of $19/mo base plus 10% of recovered money, the same
carrier recovering $500/mo pays $69/mo; recovering $0 pays $19. The
base covers the vendor's cost of serving the account; the success fee
is the alignment. DueWell's structure is the template: the higher the
base, the lower the success fee.

### 6.4 Verdict: offer it as an option, not the model

**Recommendation [hypothesis]:** launch with the subscription tiers
in section 8, and offer a success-fee plan as a named alternative for
buyers who will not subscribe before a recovery: $0/mo base, 15% of
amounts recorded as paid in ClaimTrail, billed monthly in arrears,
with the fee defined on cash recorded as paid against claims in the
system, net of refunds, and with the buyer able to see the fee
calculation on the same reports page that shows the recovered amount.
Cap the monthly success fee at the Growth price ($49) for the first
year, so the buyer never pays more than the subscription they refused;
that cap is the answer to the crossover problem, and it turns the
success-fee plan into a trial with a ceiling rather than an open-ended
tax on recovery. The cap should be reviewed after the first 20
success-fee customers, not set in stone.

Four conditions before the success-fee plan can ship, all of which are
product work, not pricing work:

1. Payment recording must be trustworthy: the fee is charged on
   amounts recorded as paid, so the record-payment flow (already
   built, v1.0.1) must be the single source of truth, and the fee
   calculation must be auditable line by line, DueWell style.
2. Attribution must be defined in writing: the fee applies to
   payments recorded against claims created in ClaimTrail, within 12
   months of the claim's creation, net of refunds. Payments recorded
   against claims created elsewhere are not charged.
3. Billing must support metered arrears: Stripe metered billing or
   manual invoicing for the success fee, which is a v1.2.0 scope item
   (see section 9). Do not promise the plan before the billing build
   can meter it.
4. The plan must not be the default. The subscription is the default;
   the success-fee plan is offered at the point where a trial buyer
   says the price is the blocker. Offering it to everyone on the
   pricing page invites adverse selection: the buyers who recover
   nothing choose it, and the buyers who recover a lot choose the
   subscription, which is exactly backwards for revenue.

## 7. The free tier and the trial: recommendation

The old plan's Free tier ($0, 1k events, 3 days) fails on every test in
this document:

- 3 days cannot contain a claim's life cycle (section 5.3).
- A permanent free tier converts at 2-5% (section 5.2) and invites
  use as a free filing cabinet.
- The limits are in units the buyer cannot judge (section 1).

**Recommendation [hypothesis]: replace the permanent Free tier with a
30-day trial, no card required, on the Growth tier, and keep a free
tier only as a narrow, deliberately limited plan.** The two offers:

- **Trial: 30 days, no card, full Growth tier.** 30 days is long
  enough to build, submit and follow up a claim, and possibly to see
  the first fast payment. It is longer than the 14-day norm, for the
  life-cycle reason in section 5.3, and the conversion benchmarks say
  a 30-day trial converts worse than a 14-day one on median (14%
  against 19% in 1Capture's data), so the trade is accepted knowingly:
  fewer conversions, but conversions of buyers who have seen the
  outcome, which is what survives the first renewal. The trial ends
  with a choice: subscribe, switch to the success-fee plan in section
  6, or drop to the free tier below.
- **Free tier: $0, 1 truck, 5 claims per month, 30 days of history,
  no submission tracking beyond the claim record, no PDF package
  export.** Deliberately narrow. It exists so a one-truck
  owner-operator can keep a record and so a trial buyer who is not
  ready can stay in the product, not so a fleet can run its claims
  for free. The 5-claim cap is the forcing function: a fleet that
  files more than 5 claims a month is a paying customer by volume.
  No card, no expiry, but also no support beyond the help docs.

The trial-to-paid target, from the benchmarks in section 5.2, is 15%
for a no-card trial as a good result, with 8% as the floor below which
the trial is failing. Those are targets for the pilot in v0.6.0 to
measure, not promises. The free-to-paid target is 3-5% as a good
result, which is why the free tier is a retention net, not the growth
engine.

One more consideration, specific to this segment: the buyer phones for
help. A free tier with phone support is a cost centre; a trial with
onboarding help is a sales cost. Support must be tiered with the plan
(see section 9), and the free tier gets documentation and community
support only.

## 8. Revised pricing hypotheses

The revised tiers, replacing the RelayDeck-era plan. All prices are
hypotheses to be tested in v0.2.0 interviews and the v0.6.0 pilot.
Monthly prices in USD; annual is 10x monthly (two months free).

| Tier | Price | Trucks | Limits (buyer units) | Who it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | 1 truck | 5 claims/mo, 1 member, 30 days history, no PDF package export | One-truck owner-operator keeping a record; trial buyers not ready to pay |
| Starter | $19/mo | 1-2 trucks | 20 claims/mo, 3 members, 1 GB storage, PDF packages, deadlines, reports | Owner-operator or 2-truck fleet that bills detention regularly |
| Growth | $49/mo | 3-10 trucks | 100 claims/mo, 10 members, 10 GB storage, everything in Starter plus custom fields, event categories, evidence templates, export templates, operations dashboard | The 3-10 truck fleet and the small dispatcher; the default tier |
| Fleet | $99/mo | 11-30 trucks | 300 claims/mo, 25 members, 50 GB storage, everything in Growth plus priority support | The 11-30 truck carrier; priced against DOCK2BILL Yard ($299) and Eden One 16-30 ($329 founder) with a large gap, which is intentional: ClaimTrail is the claim tool, not the office system |
| Agency | $149/mo base | Up to 10 carriers, then $12 per additional carrier/mo | 100 claims/mo per carrier, unlimited members across carriers, 10 GB storage per carrier, agency dashboard (v2.1.0), everything in Growth | Dispatch agencies running claims for many carriers in one account |

Notes on the table, each a hypothesis with its reasoning:

1. **Starter at $19 is kept, but its limits are rewritten.** $19 was
   the old plan's price and it undercuts CabCommand Solo ($29.99) and
   GetDockPay Pro ($24.99). The 1-2 truck band matches the buyer who
   would otherwise buy a driver app. The 20-claim monthly cap is
   generous for 2 trucks (a truck files a detention claim a few times
   a month at most, given ATRI's 39.3% of stops involve detention and
   most stops are not claimed), so the cap rarely binds and the tier
   is really a price point, not a limit. That is fine: the upgrade
   trigger is trucks and members, not claims.
2. **Growth at $49 is kept, and is now the default.** $49 undercuts
   every desk rival (Eden One $69 founder, DOCK2BILL $99, Tezify $249
   at 5 trucks) and matches Tezify's 1-truck price while covering 10
   trucks. The 3-10 truck band is the v0.1.0 beachhead (6-20 truck
   fleets, adjusted down to 3 because the data says the pain starts
   at 3 trucks with a dispatcher). The 100-claim cap is about 10
   claims per truck per month, which is above any realistic filing
   rate, so again the cap is a guardrail, not the upgrade trigger.
3. **Fleet at $99 is new.** The old plan jumped from $49 to nothing.
   A 15-truck carrier on Growth at $49 was paying $3.27 per truck,
   which is a good deal for them and a bad deal for ClaimTrail. $99
   for 11-30 trucks is $3.30-9.00 per truck, still far below Eden
   One's $329 and DOCK2BILL's $299 at the same size, and it gives the
   11-truck buyer a reason to upgrade that is not a punishment. The
   per-truck price falls with size, as it does everywhere in the
   field (section 4, point 2).
4. **Agency at $149/mo base is new and is the most important change.**
   $149 is set deliberately against DOCK2BILL Partner Pro at $149/mo,
   the only published agency number in the field. The difference is
   what the $149 buys: DOCK2BILL pays the dispatcher a commission for
   referrals; ClaimTrail gives the dispatcher an operations desk that
   runs the carriers' claims. The $12 per additional carrier beyond 10
   keeps the per-carrier price falling ($14.90 per carrier at 10,
   $13.45 at 20) while keeping the total honest: an agency with 20
   carriers pays $269/mo, against $490/mo under the old per-organization
   pricing and against $149/mo plus commission from DOCK2BILL. The
   agency tier is a hypothesis that needs the interview question in
   section 10 more than any other number in this document, because no
   agency has been asked yet.
5. **The success-fee plan (section 6) sits alongside the tiers, not
   in them:** $0/mo base, 15% of amounts recorded as paid, capped at
   $49/mo for the first year. It is offered at the trial-exit point,
   not on the pricing page.
6. **Limits are in buyer units throughout.** Trucks, claims per month,
   members, storage. The old "events" unit is dropped entirely. The
   v1.2.1 entitlement build (section 9) enforces exactly these limits:
   shipment limits, claim limits, member limits, storage limits,
   document limits, reporting restrictions.

### 8.1 What the revised tiers do to the market sizing [estimate]

v0.1.0 sized the serviceable obtainable market at 10,000 paying
organizations at an average of $39/mo, $4.7M ARR. The revised tiers
change the average, not the logic. If the paying mix is 40% Starter
($19), 40% Growth ($49), 10% Fleet ($99) and 10% Agency ($149 base,
counted per agency, not per carrier), the blended average is about
$52/mo, and 10,000 paying organizations is about $6.2M ARR. If the
mix skews to Starter, the average falls to about $35/mo and the same
10,000 organizations is $4.2M ARR. The honest range is therefore
$4-6M ARR at 10,000 paying organizations, against v0.1.0's $4.7M,
which sits inside the range. The sizing does not change materially,
because the price points did not change materially; what changed is
that the tiers now have bands, limits in buyer units, and an agency
price. The TAM ceiling from v0.1.0 ($233M ARR at Starter pricing for
1.02M micro-carriers) is unchanged as a ceiling, and is still not a
target.

### 8.2 Price changes that are not proposed

- No per-seat charge. Rejected in section 2.2.
- No price increase on Growth. $49 is the wedge against the desk
  field; raising it before validation would trade the one clear price
  advantage for unproven revenue.
- No annual-only plans. Monthly, cancel any time, is a requirement in
  a churn-heavy segment (v0.1.0), and every rival offers it.
- No setup fee. No rival charges one, and a setup fee is a reason not
  to start a trial.

## 9. Subscription structure for v1.2.0 and v1.2.1

This section is the specification the billing build works from. It
maps the revised tiers in section 8 onto the checklist items for
v1.2.0 (Subscription Billing) and v1.2.1 (Plans and Entitlements), so
the build does not have to re-decide pricing.

### 9.1 Plans (v1.2.0)

- Five plans: Free, Starter, Growth, Fleet, Agency, plus the
  success-fee plan as a non-listed alternative (section 6). Each plan
  is a Stripe product with a monthly price and an annual price at 10x
  monthly.
- Monthly and annual billing; checkout via Stripe Checkout; free
  trials (30 days, no card) handled as a Stripe trial on the Growth
  plan, converting to a paid subscription unless cancelled.
- Upgrades take effect immediately with proration; downgrades take
  effect at the end of the current billing period, with the limits of
  the lower tier applying from then (a downgrade must not delete data;
  it must block new claims above the lower limit and warn the user).
- Renewals, cancellations, invoice history, failed-payment handling
  (dunning: retry, then downgrade to Free after 14 days past due,
  never delete data), Stripe webhooks, billing portal (Stripe Customer
  Portal for payment method and invoice management).
- The success-fee plan needs metered billing: usage records for
  amounts recorded as paid, invoiced monthly in arrears, with the
  $49/mo first-year cap applied in the billing code, not as a promise
  in the marketing copy.

### 9.2 Entitlements (v1.2.1)

The limits in section 8 map to the checklist's entitlement items as
follows. Enforcement is backend, not frontend-only; the frontend
shows upgrade prompts, the backend refuses the action.

| Checklist item | Enforcement |
| --- | --- |
| Shipment limits | Per-plan monthly shipment cap, derived from the claim cap: shipments are not capped directly, because a shipment is not the billed unit; the claim cap is the binding limit. Record this decision: shipment caps were considered and rejected, because capping shipments would stop a carrier recording loads they have already run. |
| Claim limits | Hard cap per plan per month (Free 5, Starter 20, Growth 100, Fleet 300, Agency 100 per carrier). Creating a claim above the cap returns 402 with an upgrade prompt. Claims already created are never deleted by a cap. |
| Organization member limits | Free 1, Starter 3, Growth 10, Fleet 25, Agency unlimited across carriers. Invitations above the cap are refused with an upgrade prompt. |
| Storage limits | Free 100 MB, Starter 1 GB, Growth 10 GB, Fleet 50 GB, Agency 10 GB per carrier. Uploads above the limit are refused with an upgrade prompt. Storage is measured on evidence files and claim packages. |
| Document limits | PDF package exports: Free none, all paid tiers unlimited. Evidence files per claim: no hard cap, governed by the storage limit. |
| Reporting restrictions | Free: dashboard only. Starter and above: full reports. Agency: consolidated cross-carrier reporting is a v2.1.0 feature, not a v1.2.1 gate. |
| Backend entitlement enforcement | A single entitlement service consulted by every creation endpoint (claims, invitations, uploads). No endpoint enforces limits by reading the plan name directly. |
| Usage tracking | Per-organization monthly counters for claims created, storage used, members, and (for the success-fee plan) amounts recorded as paid. Visible to the organization owner on the billing page, and to the vendor in the operations dashboard. |
| Upgrade prompts | The frontend shows the prompt at the point of the refused action, naming the limit hit and the tier that removes it. No generic "upgrade" banner without the number. |

### 9.3 What billing must not do

- Never charge for a claim that was not collected on the success-fee
  plan. The fee is on amounts recorded as paid, net of refunds.
- Never delete data on downgrade, cancellation or failed payment.
  Downgrade to Free preserves everything and blocks new creation
  above the Free limits.
- Never require a card for the trial. The card-required trial converts
  at about 49% against about 18% for no-card (section 5.2), but the
  segment distrusts card-upfront offers, and the no-card trial is the
  trust position for a product that has no brand yet. Revisit after
  the pilot measures actual conversion.
- Never price in "events". The billing code, the pricing page and the
  upgrade prompts all use trucks, claims, members and storage.

## 10. What v0.2.0 interviews must still test

This version is validated by proxy only. The following questions are
for v0.2.0, in the order that matters, with the kill criteria that
would force a re-price. Add them to the v0.2.0 question list when
interviews start; do not treat any price in section 8 as settled
until they are answered.

### 10.1 Interview questions

1. Do you bill detention today, and what share of what you bill do
   you actually collect? (Sets the value math in section 3 against
   their real numbers.)
2. What do you use now: paper, a TMS field, a driver app, a
   dispatcher? What do you pay for it, per month, in money or in
   time? (Sets the anchor in section 4 against their real spend.)
3. Would you pay $19/mo for the claim workflow if it could not send
   the claim to the broker? What if it could? (Tests Starter, and
   tests whether the missing submission channel is a price problem
   or a product problem.)
4. Would you pay $49/mo for a desk that runs detention, layover,
   TONU and lumper claims for 3-10 trucks, with evidence checks,
   deadlines and a PDF package? What would have to be true for $49
   to feel cheap? (Tests Growth, and surfaces the condition that
   makes the price acceptable.)
5. If you are a dispatch agency: how many carriers do you run claims
   for, and what would you pay per carrier per month for one account
   that runs all of them? Is $149/mo for up to 10 carriers with $12
   per extra carrier a number you would sign, or a number you would
   laugh at? (Tests the Agency tier, the least validated number in
   this document.)
6. Would you rather pay $49/mo flat, or $0/mo plus 15% of what you
   actually collect, capped at $49/mo? Which one feels safer, and
   why? (Tests the success-fee alternative in section 6.)
7. How long would you need to try it before deciding: 7 days, 14,
   30, or until you see a claim paid? Would you give a card upfront
   for the trial? (Tests the trial design in section 7.)
8. What would make you stop paying: a month with no recoveries, a
   price rise, a broker who still does not pay, or something else?
   (Tests churn triggers, which set the dunning and retention design
   in section 9.)
9. Do you check a broker's payment history before booking a load,
   and would a shared broker-pay score change which loads you take?
   (Carried from v0.4.0; decides whether broker intelligence is a
   priced feature or a free one.)
10. Have you tried GetDockPay, Detention Buddy, TruckersClaims,
    DOCK2BILL, Eden One, Run Your Rig or Tezify's detention features,
    and why did you stop or not pay? (Carried from v0.4.0; the answer
    prices ClaimTrail against the product they already rejected.)

### 10.2 Kill criteria

- If most interviewees say they would not pay more than $10/mo for
  any detention software, the desk tiers are mispriced and the
  success-fee plan becomes the primary model, with the subscription
  as the cap. Re-price before v1.2.0.
- If most interviewees say they will only pay on recovery, the
  subscription launch is premature and the success-fee plan should
  launch first, with the subscription added once the product has
  paying success-fee customers to convert.
- If agencies say they will not pay per carrier at any number, the
  agency beachhead is wrong and the $149 tier is dropped; the agency
  segment is then served by the Growth tier per organization, and the
  v0.1.0 beachhead hypothesis is revised.
- If interviewees say 30 days is not enough to judge the product and
  they need to see a claim paid, the trial must extend to the first
  paid claim (a "pay when it bills clean" trial) rather than a fixed
  30 days.
- If interviewees already pay a TMS $145+/mo and see no reason to add
  a standalone tool, the standalone buyer is narrower than the
  beachhead assumes, and the pricing must be tested against
  non-TMS carriers only.
