# ClaimTrail v0.1.0 — Market Research

Phase 0, Business Validation. Researched 2026-11-21 from public sources
listed in §7. Figures marked **[sourced]** come from the cited source;
figures marked **[estimate]** are ClaimTrail's own arithmetic on sourced
inputs and are hypotheses to be tested in v0.2.0–v0.5.0, not facts.
Nothing in this document is based on interviews: no customer has been
spoken to yet. That is v0.2.0's job.

## 1. Trucking revenue leakage

### 1.1 The headline numbers

The American Transportation Research Institute (ATRI) published the
most recent comprehensive detention study in September 2024, based on
2023 data from 587 drivers and 245 motor carriers **[sourced]**:

- Drivers were detained at **39.3% of all stops** in 2023.
- Total detention across for-hire trucking exceeded **135 million hours**
  in 2023.
- Cost: **$3.6 billion in direct expenses** plus **$11.5 billion in lost
  productivity**, a combined **$15.1 billion** in 2023.
- Drivers lost **117–209 hours per driver per year** to detention,
  depending on sector (reefer highest at 209.4 hours; truckload 173 hours;
  specialized 117 hours).
- **94.5% of fleets charge detention fees, but fewer than 50% of those
  invoices are paid.**
- Detention rates by segment: refrigerated trailers **56.2%** of stops,
  spot-market fleets **42.5%**, truckload dry van 32.6%, flatbed 26.2%,
  specialized 19.3%. Detentions over two hours occurred in 9.9% of stops.
- Detained trucks drove **14.6% faster on average** than trucks that had
  not been detained — detention is also a safety cost.
- 72 million gallons of diesel were wasted idling during detention in 2023.

The U.S. Department of Transportation's Office of Inspector General
(January 2018, Report ST2018019) estimated detention is associated with
**$1.1–1.3 billion a year in reduced earnings** for for-hire truckload
drivers — **$1,281–$1,534 per driver per year** — and reduces motor
carrier net income by **$250.6–302.9 million annually** **[sourced]**.
That report is eight years old and rests on FMCSA's 2014 detention data,
so treat its dollar figures as a floor from an earlier market, not a
current estimate.

Supporting findings **[sourced]**:

- OOIDA's 2023 survey: **72% of owner-operators and small-fleet drivers
  waited over two hours weekly**; 17% of drivers never receive any
  detention pay, and of those who always request it, 9% say they never
  actually receive payment.
- DAT: **fewer than 50% of billed detention charges are actually paid**
  by shippers.
- GetDockPay, citing ATRI 2024: carriers bill detention only **75% of
  the time**, and only 55% of billed invoices are paid — i.e. roughly
  **41% of detention owed is ever collected** (0.75 × 0.55). That
  multiplication is ours **[estimate]**; the two inputs are ATRI's.
- The same pattern shows in the trade press: uncollected accessorial
  charges represent **$3,000–$10,000 per year in lost revenue** for a
  typical owner-operator (USA Trucker Choice, trade guide, not a study)
  **[trade estimate]**.

### 1.2 Why the money leaks

Four distinct leaks, each a product requirement:

1. **Never billed.** The carrier does not know the charge exists, or the
   driver never reports the wait. Small carriers run on notebooks and
   memory; there is no automatic capture of arrival, dock-in and
   departure times.
2. **Billed late or without proof.** The invoice goes out days later with
   no timestamps, no signed BOL, no rate confirmation attached. The
   broker disputes the times with their own record and the claim dies.
   ATRI's own finding is that the shipper usually holds the time record.
3. **Billed but never chased.** No follow-up schedule, no escalation, no
   deadline tracking. A claim that is not followed up on day 3, 7 and 14
   is a claim that is forgotten.
4. **Never billed on purpose.** Carriers skip billing to protect a broker
   relationship — ATRI found this concern is strongest for small
   carriers, who depend on a handful of brokers. A product cannot fix
   the relationship fear, but it can lower the cost of billing to near
   zero, which changes the trade-off.

ClaimTrail's Phase 1 scope already maps to leaks 1–3: shipment events
with timestamps, evidence attached to claims, PDF claim packages,
deadlines with automated reminders, and claim status tracking through
payment. Leak 4 is a positioning and pricing problem, not a feature.

### 1.3 What "leakage" means for ClaimTrail's value claim

The honest value claim, from sourced numbers only:

- A truckload driver loses ~173 hours a year to detention. At the ATRI
  average charged rate of ~$63/hour, that is ~$10,900 a year of
  detention value per driver, of which (75% billed × 55% paid) roughly
  $4,500 is collected and ~$6,400 is not **[estimate built on sourced
  inputs]**.
- A 5-truck carrier therefore has on the order of **$30,000 a year** of
  uncollected detention value on the table **[estimate]**. If ClaimTrail
  helped recover even a fifth of it, that is ~$6,000 a year against a
  subscription of a few hundred dollars. That ratio is the sales story —
  but it is an estimate until interviews confirm real recovery rates
  (v0.2.0, v0.5.0).

## 2. Missed accessorial charges

Accessorial charges are fees beyond the base line-haul rate for time,
equipment, special handling or difficult locations. The charges
ClaimTrail already models (detention, layover, TONU, lumper, additional
stops, additional mileage, custom events) cover the highest-value ones.
Typical market rates, from trade sources **[sourced, ranges vary by
contract and region]**:

| Charge | Typical rate | Trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detention | $50–$75/hr (ATRI avg charged ~$63/hr; range $25–$100) | After 2 free hours at shipper/receiver |
| Layover | $250–$350/day (range $150–$500) | Forced overnight wait to next day |
| TONU (Truck Order Not Used) | $200–$300 (range $150–$600) | Load cancelled after dispatch / same-day |
| Lumper fee | $150–$250 (range $50–$350) | Third-party unloading at warehouse/DC |
| Driver assist | $50–$150 | Driver helps load/unload |
| Additional stops | $50–$200 each | Delivery beyond the first stop |
| Redelivery / 2nd trip | ~$400 per occurrence (range $0–$500) | First delivery attempt failed |
| Liftgate | $75–$100 (range $30–$150) | No dock available |
| Tarp | $100–$150 (range $75–$200) | Flatbed weather protection |
| Hazmat | $150–$200 (range $150–$300) | Hazardous materials |
| Residential / limited access | $75–$125 | Non-commercial delivery location |

Notes:

- Detention is the most common truckload accessorial and the largest
  single leakage pool. Layover and TONU are the next largest per
  occurrence. Lumper is common at grocery and big-retail distribution
  centers and is frequently paid out of pocket and never reclaimed.
- The average detention rate charged (~$63/hr) is **below the average
  operating cost of $66.65/hr** (ATRI) — carriers underprice even the
  detention they do bill. A product that pre-fills the contract rate
  from the rate confirmation removes one more reason to under-bill.
- Free time is usually 2 hours (67% of carriers per ATRI); some rate
  confirmations specify 1 or 3 hours. ClaimTrail's per-organization
  deadline settings already handle this variation.
- Charges outside ClaimTrail's current event model (liftgate, tarp,
  hazmat, residential) are handled today as custom events. Whether they
  deserve first-class event types is a v0.3.0 workflow-validation
  question, not a v0.1.0 decision.

## 3. Target customer segments

### 3.1 Market size and shape

The U.S. for-hire trucking market **[sourced]**:

- **$906 billion** gross freight revenue in 2024, **11.27 billion tons**,
  trucks moving 72.7% of U.S. freight by weight (American Trucking
  Associations).
- **~580,000 active motor carriers** registered with FMCSA as of June
  2025, owning or leasing at least one tractor; **91.5% operate 10 or
  fewer trucks** and 99.3% operate 100 or fewer (FMCSA via ATA).
- A separate FMCSA census read (November 14, 2026) counts **1,111,627
  active carriers authorized for hire reporting at least one truck**,
  of which **66% run a single truck and 92% run five or fewer**; average
  fleet 3.7 trucks. The two counts differ because they count different
  populations (all active motor carriers vs. authorized-for-hire
  carriers with trucks on file). Both agree on the shape: a very long
  tail of tiny carriers.
- **3.58 million professional drivers** in 2024 (ATA).
- Average operating cost hit a record **$2.336 per mile** in 2025 (ATRI
  2026 report) — margins are thin, so recovered accessorials matter
  more, not less.
- Churn is high: 97,209 carriers were new in the last 12 months
  (FMCSA census read, Nov 2026), and the 1–6 truck tier has been
  shrinking since a 2023 peak (tractors ~398,000 down to ~362,000,
  FreightWaves/SONAR). Small carriers fail often; a subscription
  business in this segment must expect churn and price accordingly.

### 3.2 Segments, ranked for ClaimTrail

| Segment | Size (sourced) | Why they fit | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1–5 truck owner-operators and micro-fleets** | ~1.02M carriers (92% of 1.11M authorized-for-hire) | Largest pool; no back office; detention hits them hardest per truck; cheapest to reach online | Highest churn; most price-sensitive; many run on paper and phones, not desktops |
| **6–20 truck small fleets** | ~67,000 carriers | Have a dispatcher/office person who feels the paperwork pain daily; can pay $49–$99/mo; multi-user fits ClaimTrail's RBAC | Small absolute count; many already on a TMS |
| **21–100 truck fleets** | ~18,500 carriers | Real back-office cost; claim volume justifies a dedicated tool; likely to need integrations | Longer sales cycle; expect TMS/ELD integrations and SLAs ClaimTrail does not have yet |
| **Dispatch agencies / dispatch services** | Not counted in carrier census; dozens-to-hundreds of agencies, each managing 5–50 carriers | **Best fit for multi-tenancy**: one organization per carrier is exactly ClaimTrail's model; they already fight for detention pay as a service and bill per carrier | Agencies may build or white-label their own tooling; they need per-carrier reporting, which is Phase 7 agency work |
| **Reefer and spot-market operators** | Reefer detained at 56.2% of stops; spot fleets 42.5% (ATRI) | Highest detention frequency = highest recoverable value per truck; the wedge segment inside the micro-fleet pool | Spot operators are the most transient and hardest to retain |

### 3.3 Recommended beachhead (hypothesis for v0.2.0 to test)

**Dispatch agencies and 2–20 truck fleets running reefer or spot freight.**
Reasons: they have someone whose job already includes chasing detention
(the buyer is identifiable), the per-truck value is highest, and the
multi-tenant model is a genuine advantage over driver-phone apps. The
1-truck owner-operator is the volume play for later, once the product
has a mobile-friendly capture flow and a price point under ~$25/mo —
ClaimTrail today is a desktop web app, so the single-truck driver is
not the first buyer.

What would disprove this: if interviews show dispatch agencies already
recover detention through their TMS or factoring company and see no gap,
or if 2–20 truck fleets will not pay for a standalone tool when their
TMS already has a detention field. Both are v0.2.0 interview questions.

## 4. Existing solutions

This is a market-research pass, not the full competitor analysis —
that is v0.4.0, which starts from the 2026-11-14 DockClock competitor
check (GetDockPay, TruckersClaims, Detention Buddy, DOCK2BILL) and
re-verifies each product live. What this pass establishes is the shape
of the competitive field:

### 4.1 Driver-phone detention apps (the direct names)

- **GetDockPay** (getdockpay.com; iPhone/Android; free plan, Pro
  $24.99/mo or $239.99/yr): GPS-stamped arrival/dock-in/departure,
  detention math, PDF invoice with map, emailed notices on day 3/7/14,
  broker and dock pay scores. Cites ATRI 2024 on its own pages.
  Driver-facing, single-driver, detention-only.
- **Detention Buddy** (detentionbuddy.com; Pro $34.99/yr): GPS time
  tracking, photo evidence, PDF invoices, earnings dashboard. Claims
  $234,000+ recovered by drivers (self-reported, unverified).
  Driver-facing, detention-only, cheapest in the field.
- **TruckersClaims / DOCK2BILL**: named in the 2026-11-14 competitor
  check; details to be re-verified in v0.4.0, not re-asserted here.

### 4.2 TMS platforms with detention billing built in

- **Tezify TMS** (from $99/mo, 30 days free on up to 2 trucks): full TMS
  (dispatch, ELD, IFTA, invoicing, settlements) with a detention timer
  and detention/lumper/layover landing on the invoice automatically.
  The "detention billing" checkbox exists inside a much larger product.
- **TenTrucks** (5–50 truck fleets): AI TMS with dispatch, ELD, IFTA,
  invoicing, driver settlements. No detention-claim workflow called
  out as a headline feature.
- The pattern: TMS vendors treat detention as one invoicing line among
  dozens. None of them is a claim-recovery workflow (evidence
  requirements, readiness checklist, deadlines, follow-up schedule,
  claim packages, acceptance/rejection tracking) — that is the gap
  ClaimTrail occupies. Whether carriers will buy a standalone tool for
  that gap instead of using the TMS field they already pay for is the
  central v0.2.0 question.

### 4.3 Human services

- Dispatch services (e.g. AT Softy LLC) explicitly advertise fighting
  for detention, layover and TONU pay as part of back-office dispatch.
  Factoring companies also chase unpaid invoices. These are service
  substitutes: a carrier paying a dispatcher 5–10% of revenue already
  has someone chasing detention, badly or well. ClaimTrail's pitch to
  that segment is the tool the dispatcher uses, not a replacement for
  the dispatcher.
- No dominant, carrier-desk, multi-tenant, evidence-and-deadline
  claim platform was found in this pass. Absence of evidence is not
  evidence of absence — v0.4.0 must confirm it with a structured sweep.

## 5. Market opportunities

### 5.1 Opportunity sizing (estimates — hypotheses, not forecasts)

Bottom-up, using sourced segment sizes and ClaimTrail's planned pricing
(Free $0/1k events/3 days, Starter $19/50k/30 days, Growth $49/500k/90
days — set in the RelayDeck-era plan, to be validated in v0.5.0):

- **Serviceable obtainable market (3-year, estimate):** 10,000 paying
  organizations at an average of $39/mo = **$4.7M ARR**. That is ~1%
  of the ~1.02M micro-carriers, or ~15% of the 67,000 6–20 truck fleets.
  Reaching 10,000 paying customers in a churn-heavy, low-trust,
  word-of-mouth market is a multi-year sales effort, not a launch
  assumption.
- **Serviceable available market (estimate):** 85,000 organizations
  (6–20 truck fleets plus dispatch agencies) × $39/mo × 12 =
  **~$40M ARR** if every one of them paid. They will not; this is a
  ceiling, not a target.
- **Total addressable market (estimate):** 1.02M micro-carriers × $19/mo
  × 12 = **~$233M ARR** at Starter pricing. A ceiling only. The
  realistic read is the SOM line above.
- Sanity check against the leakage pool: $15.1B a year of detention cost
  (ATRI) is the problem's size, not the market's size. ClaimTrail's
  revenue is a subscription, not a share of recovered detention. Do not
  quote $15.1B as a TAM.

### 5.2 Where the openings are

1. **The claim workflow, not the timer.** Driver apps own time capture;
   TMS owns invoicing. Nobody owns the middle: evidence completeness,
   readiness, deadlines, follow-ups, acceptance tracking, and a
   defensible PDF package. That middle is ClaimTrail Phase 1.
2. **The dispatch-agency desk.** Multi-tenancy is ClaimTrail's
   structural advantage. One agency managing 20 carriers in one
   account is a $49–$99/mo customer with 20× the claim volume of a
   single carrier, and agencies are reachable (they advertise).
3. **Reefer and spot freight.** Highest detention rates, highest
   per-truck value, most acute pain. Targeted outreach beats generic
   "trucking software" marketing.
4. **The proof gap.** Brokers dispute times with their own records.
   GPS-stamped, timestamped evidence attached to a claim package is
   the feature brokers cannot argue with — but ClaimTrail's evidence
   today is manual uploads, not GPS capture. A mobile capture flow
   (driver taps arrival/departure, GPS stamped) is the most valuable
   future feature identified by this research; it is not in any
   checklist version yet and should be proposed for a Phase 2/4 slot
   after validation.
5. **Deadlines as a wedge.** Detention claims have contractual deadlines
   (often 30–60 days from delivery). Carriers that miss the window lose
   the claim entirely. Automated deadline tracking with reminders is a
   feature no driver app and no TMS markets as its core — and it is
   already built.

### 5.3 Risks and what would kill the thesis

- **Willingness to pay is unproven.** The cheapest competitor is
  $2.92/mo (Detention Buddy annual). A $19–$49/mo desk tool must prove
  10× the value of a $3/mo phone app. v0.5.0 must test this directly.
- **Churn.** The target segment fails often and changes TMS/dispatchers
  often. Monthly pricing with no contract is a requirement, not a choice.
- **TMS absorption.** If a major TMS adds a real claim workflow,
  ClaimTrail's standalone wedge narrows to agencies and non-TMS users.
- **Relationship fear.** Carriers that will not bill a broker to protect
  the relationship will not buy a billing tool. The addressable market
  is carriers willing to bill, not all carriers.
- **Broker power.** Even a perfect claim package can be ignored; fewer
  than half of detention invoices are paid today, and better paperwork
  alone may move that to 60%, not 100%. Recovery-rate claims in
  marketing must stay modest and sourced.
- **Single-sided market.** ClaimTrail helps carriers; brokers and
  shippers are the payers and have no reason to adopt it. Growth is
  carrier-by-carrier, not network-effect.

## 8. Limitations of this research

- No interviews, no pilot customers, no primary data. Every conclusion
  about willingness to pay, workflow fit and price points is a
  hypothesis until v0.2.0 and v0.5.0.
- ATRI's study is the best available detention data and it is 2023
  data published September 2024. The market has moved since (rates,
  diesel at record highs in November 2026, small-fleet contraction).
- Carrier counts differ between sources (580,000 vs. 1,111,627)
  because they count different populations; both are reported with
  their definitions rather than reconciled.
- Competitor prices and features are from public pages accessed
  2026-11-21 and change without notice. TruckersClaims and DOCK2BILL
  were not re-opened in this pass.
- The TAM/SAM/SOM figures in §5.1 are ClaimTrail's own arithmetic on
  sourced inputs, labelled as estimates. They are a sizing sketch, not
  a forecast.
